# Content ideas: sensitive-skin brand · Nov 23 2026

**The short version:** 3 of 5 ideas passed the checks. Each stands on a pattern that over-performs in sensitive-skin and routine videos this month, and each names the measured videos it's built on.
- The strongest is the hands-on cheat sheet: demos are 10.1% of breakouts but 4.9% of posts (index 2.06×), and their share of posts has halved since Jul–Oct.
- The other two hand the comments section a job: a question, or a call to the experts.

**Post first:** the comment question (question hooks went from 6.3% to 8.5% of posts since Jul–Oct, and still over-index at 1.15×).

## 1. Ask the comments what their skin tolerates
- **The idea:** the founder, selfie-style, in a bathroom or bedroom, asks one question about reactive skin and lets the comments answer it. The next video replies to the best answer.
- **Built on:**
  - Question hooks are climbing: 6.3% of posts in Jul–Oct, 8.5% now, and 1.15× as common among breakouts as among all posts.
  - [@nikkidoesskincare's "acne girls… whats the most random thing that helped clear your acne?"](https://www.tiktok.com/@nikkidoesskincare/video/7702098491606043935): 686K views, 606× outlier, 3,016 comments. A one-off: no other post on her page reached 10×.
- **Why now:** the share of question-led videos in the niche is rising, and they still break out more often than the average post.
- **Why it works:** a question about what *their* skin puts up with is easy to answer from experience, so people comment, and every comment is a lead for the reply video.
- **Format · length:** selfie talking head with the question on screen, 10–15 s (the source is 12 s).
- **Film it:** 1. Close selfie, founder looks into the lens, question in large text. 2. Hold up the one product she's unsure about, label to camera. 3. Back to her face, a shrug, "tell me below" on screen. · on screen: "Sensitive skin people: what's the one product your face never fights?"
- **Opening line:** "Sensitive skin people: what's the one product your face never fights?" (modelled on @nikkidoesskincare: "acne girls... whats the most random thing that helped clear your acne?")
- **For a brand account:** ask about the category, not your product. If your product comes up in the answers, reply to that comment; don't plant it.

## 2. Call in the experts
- **The idea:** the founder asks estheticians and dermatologists, by name of profession, to settle one question she's unsure about, and promises to follow what they say.
- **Built on:**
  - Direct-address hooks are over: 6.8% of breakouts against 4.7% of posts, an index of 1.46×.
  - [@alaynachristineee's "all estheticians come to the front & tell me what i need to do"](https://www.tiktok.com/@alaynachristineee/video/7705998022311693581): 2.6M live views, 46× outlier, 3,000 comments.
  - The post before it, ["how old would you say i look?"](https://www.tiktok.com/@alaynachristineee/video/7705904482567195918), hit 4.2M (75×) with 17,200 comments. 10 of the 13 other posts on her page earned under 0.5% likes per view, so the multiples here are conservative.
- **Why now:** direct address breaks out about 1.5 times as often as the niche average, while its share of posts has slipped from 5.6% to 4.7%: few people use it.
- **Why it works:** it hands viewers with real expertise a reason to show it, and viewers without it come for the argument in the comments.
- **Format · length:** selfie talking head, 10–15 s (the source is 13 s).
- **Film it:** 1. Selfie, founder holding two bottles, one in each hand. 2. Turn each label to camera for a beat. 3. Back to her face: "I'll do what the top comment says." · on screen: "Estheticians, I need you in the comments: is [ingredient] ever okay on reactive skin?"
- **Opening line:** "Estheticians, I need you in the comments: is [ingredient] ever okay on reactive skin?" (modelled on @alaynachristineee: "all estheticians come to the front & tell me what i need to do bc i'm 27 with people telling me i look 55😳")
- **For a brand account:** pick a question you genuinely haven't settled, and post the follow-up with what the experts said, even if it's against your own product.

## 3. The one-concern cheat sheet
- **The idea:** a printed chart on the table with four kinds of sensitive (redness, tightness, stinging, flaking). Under each, one ingredient to look for and one to avoid, with your products only in the rows they're for. Hands only.
- **Built on:**
  - Activity demonstrations (hands doing it) are 10.1% of this month's sensitive-skin breakouts but 4.9% of posts, an index of 2.06×.
  - [@dauixgpzgzu's acne cheat sheet](https://www.tiktok.com/@dauixgpzgzu/video/7705843182390480141): 2.6M views, 1,902× outlier, measured today in the niche-breakouts run. 2 more posts on her page hit 10×+, one of them a repost of the same chart.
  - The source is acne, not sensitive skin: what changes is the rows (types of reaction instead of types of breakout).
- **Why now:** fewer people make demos: their share of posts fell from 9.9% (Jul–Oct) to 4.9%, while they break out about twice as often as the average post. That's the under-supplied opening.
- **Why it works:** it's a reference. People save it and come back to find their own row.
- **Format · length:** tabletop, hands only, no voiceover, 20–30 s (the source is 26 s).
- **Film it:** 1. Top-down: the blank chart, four row labels. 2. Hands place one ingredient card and one "avoid" card in each row. 3. Hands set a product in the one row it fits; empty rows stay empty. · on screen: "Red, tight, stinging, flaky: four kinds of sensitive, four things to look for."
- **Opening line:** "Red, tight, stinging, flaky: four kinds of sensitive, four things to look for." (modelled on @dauixgpzgzu's caption: "Not all acne is the same! 🚨")
- **For a brand account:** no result claims on the chart: ingredients and what they're for, not "clears" or "fixes". A row with none of your products is what makes it believable.
